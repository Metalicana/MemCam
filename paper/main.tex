\title{\textbf{The Archive Is Not the Context:}\\
Diagnosing and Curating Memory for Long-Horizon Video Generation}
\author{Anonymous authors}
\date{}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
External-memory video generators have focused on retrieval while allowing the
underlying memory bank to grow with video duration. This creates linear storage,
quadratic cumulative candidate search, and an increasingly crowded retrieval
pool, even though the generator consumes only a fixed-size context. We show
that this design fails both computationally and statistically. On fifteen
180-second camera-controlled rollouts, a 32-frame Geometric Coverage bank
reduces FVD from 734.2 to 476.6 and LPIPS from 0.5980 to 0.5876 relative to
unbounded memory. We decompose the failure into target-view mismatch and
corruption of the stored generated image. As history grows, view mismatch
improves, but selected-memory corruption increases by 0.087 DINO distance
(95\% CI: 0.024--0.157), while effective conditioning deteriorates. A
common-source control changes only selected indices: Geometric Coverage chooses
memories that are 4.63 dB cleaner in PSNR and 0.151 higher in SSIM than the
unbounded selector, winning on all fifteen trajectories. Motivated by this
diagnosis, we propose \method: a constant-memory update rule that rejects
unreliable observations, protects rare content, and maximizes geometric view
coverage without requiring future camera poses. Our study reframes memory
curation as part of the generative error-control loop, rather than only a
systems optimization.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Long-horizon video generation turns a finite-context model into a recurrent
system. The generator produces a chunk, stores information from that chunk,
and retrieves historical information to condition the next one. This pattern
appears in RGB-frame memory \citep{contextasmemory,memcam}, learned latent
memory \citep{worldmem,mirage}, surfel-indexed view memory \citep{vmem}, and
point-cloud memory \citep{spatia}. External memory addresses forgetting, but
it introduces a second problem: what should remain available when history is
longer than the useful memory budget?

The default answer in several systems is to keep everything. Storage and
candidate-search cost then grow with video duration, but the generator does
not consume all archived items. It receives a small retrieved context. An
unbounded archive therefore increases the number of candidates competing for
a fixed number of conditioning slots. The archive and the context are
different objects.

We first study the consequences of this distinction in MemCam
\citep{memcam}, an open camera-controlled generator that stores decoded RGB
frames and retrieves one historical frame for each target frame using camera
field-of-view overlap. We leave the generator and retriever unchanged and
intervene only on the candidate bank. Surprisingly, simple fixed-budget
curation substantially outperforms the complete archive at long horizons. At
180 seconds and a 32-frame budget, rarity-irreplaceability (RI) improves FVD
from 734.2 to 550.4, while Geometric Coverage reaches 476.6.
The latter is a 35.1\% reduction despite retaining less than one percent of
the generated frames.

Our main goal is not to declare that a particular heuristic is universally
optimal. It is to determine what unbounded memory gets wrong. We build a
diagnostic decomposition using exact trajectory-indexed ground-truth frames.
The decomposition separates whether a selected index depicts the desired view
from whether the generated image stored at that index is itself corrupted. We
find that view alignment improves as the archive grows, while the visual
integrity of selected memories deteriorates. A common-source experiment then
holds image content fixed to the same unbounded rollout and evaluates only the
indices selected by each policy. RI and Geometric Coverage still select substantially cleaner
indices, establishing that curation changes the selector's exposure rather
than merely benefiting from an already-cleaner generated history.

Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item We identify a counter-intuitive failure of external memory in
    long-horizon video generation: increasing the archive can degrade the
    fixed-size retrieved context and the final video.
    \item We introduce a retrieval-quality decomposition that separates view
    mismatch, stored-image corruption, retention loss, and selection loss.
    The reference is explicitly a hindsight diagnostic, not a claimed
    ground-truth useful-memory oracle.
    \item Through matched 180-second rollouts and a common-source control, we
    show that RI and especially Geometric Coverage systematically select
    cleaner generated evidence. We also report negative mechanism tests that
    rule out four plausible alternatives.
    \item Motivated by the diagnosis, we propose \method, which makes
    reliability an admission rule, rare-content retention a constraint, and
    geometric coverage the primary fixed-budget objective.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{Long-horizon generation with memory.}
Context-as-Memory stores historical RGB frames and retrieves them using camera
field-of-view overlap \citep{contextasmemory}. MemCam adds context compression
and an open implementation of related RGB-frame retrieval
\citep{memcam}. WorldMem stores memory frames together with pose and timestamp
states and reads them through memory attention \citep{worldmem}. VMem indexes
past views using surfels \citep{vmem}; Spatia maintains an updatable point
cloud \citep{spatia}; and Mirage stores spatial evidence directly in diffusion
latent space \citep{mirage}. AnchorWeave retrieves multiple local geometric
memories to avoid errors from globally fused reconstruction
\citep{anchorweave}. These works primarily study how memory should be
represented and retrieved. We study how a continually growing memory should
be \emph{curated} when the future trajectory is unknown.

\paragraph{Bounded and adaptive memory.}
Surprise Forcing treats memory admission and denoising as online resource
allocation using novelty and feedback-controlled thresholds
\citep{surpriseforcing}. In language models, bounded KV-cache methods and
submodular summarization similarly retain a compact historical subset
\citep{bumblebee}. Classical subset selection provides coverage and diversity
objectives with diminishing returns \citep{nemhauser1978,badanidiyuru2014}.
Our present study does not claim a new approximation theorem. Instead, it uses
two transparent online curation rules as interventions and focuses on the
previously unmeasured failure mechanism of generated visual memory.

\paragraph{Video-generation evaluation.}
We report LPIPS \citep{lpips} and FVD \citep{fvd} for perceptual and
distributional quality. VBench \citep{vbench} measures subject and background
consistency, motion smoothness, dynamic degree, aesthetic quality, and imaging
quality. CUT3R \citep{cut3r} reconstructs the generated camera trajectory so
that camera-control rotation and translation errors can be measured against
the commanded path. We use PSNR/SSIM \citep{ssim} only where exact
trajectory-indexed ground truth makes pixel comparison meaningful. DINOv2
features \citep{dinov2} support diagnostic appearance distances, but
DINO-based diagnostics are not treated as true utility labels.

\section{Why Unbounded Memory Fails}
\label{sec:formulation}

\subsection{Streaming generation}

At chunk $t$, let $Y_t$ denote the frames to generate, $P_t$ the known camera
poses for that chunk, and $M_{t-1}$ the persistent memory bank. A retriever
$R$ maps each target pose to a small context:
\begin{align}
    C_t &= R(P_t; M_{t-1}), \\
    \hat{Y}_t &= G(\hat{Y}_{t-1}, P_t, C_t), \\
    M_t &= U(M_{t-1}, N_t; B),
\end{align}
where $N_t$ contains newly generated memory candidates and $B$ is the archive
budget. Unbounded memory sets $B=\infty$ and appends every generated frame.
The camera trajectory beyond the current chunk is unavailable to $U$.

This distinction matters computationally. If each chunk adds $s$ candidates,
then storage is $O(Ts)$ after $T$ chunks. Exhaustive retrieval compares the
current targets with all prior candidates, giving $O(T^2s)$ cumulative
candidate scoring. A fixed bank gives $O(B)$ storage and $O(TB)$ cumulative
retrieval. More importantly, candidate growth changes the statistical problem
faced by $R$ even when the number of returned memories is fixed.

Figure~\ref{fig:problem} makes the failure concrete. In the current MemCam
implementation, decoded-bank storage grows from 0.38 GB at 10 seconds to
2.30 GB at 60 seconds; implementation-calibrated extrapolation reaches 22.7 GB
at 10 minutes and 136 GB at 60 minutes. Because every target is compared with
all prior candidates, cumulative retrieval grows quadratically. The same
unbounded runs also show monotonic LPIPS degradation from 0.4886 at 10 seconds
to 0.5980 at 180 seconds. FVD is noisier but remains high rather than improving
with additional history. Storage values follow the implemented representation;
long-duration latency points are extrapolations and are not presented as
hardware measurements.

\subsection{MemCam retrieval}

MemCam estimates camera FOV overlap through Monte Carlo visibility tests and
selects the maximum-overlap historical frame for each target
\citep{memcam}. All methods in our controlled study use this same frozen
retriever, generator, prompts, camera paths, seeds, and denoising schedule.
Only $U$, the bank-update rule, changes.

\subsection{A diagnostic decomposition}

The dataset provides a ground-truth image $x_i^{\GT}$ at each trajectory index
$i$, while the model stores its generated image $\hat{x}_i$. For a target index
$q$ and selected memory index $i$, define
\begin{align}
 d_{\mathrm{view}}(i,q)
   &= d_{\DINO}(x_i^{\GT}, x_q^{\GT}), \\
 d_{\mathrm{corr}}(i)
   &= d_{\DINO}(\hat{x}_i, x_i^{\GT}), \\
 d_{\mathrm{eff}}(i,q)
   &= d_{\DINO}(\hat{x}_i, x_q^{\GT}).
\end{align}
View mismatch asks whether the index depicts a related view. Corruption asks
whether the stored generated image is faithful at its own index. Effective
mismatch measures the actual generated evidence supplied for the target.

For diagnosis only, we define the hindsight-best candidate over complete
history $\Hset_{t-1}$ and over the retained bank $M_{t-1}$:
\begin{align}
 i^*_{\Hset}(q) &= \arg\min_{i\in\Hset_{t-1}} d_{\mathrm{eff}}(i,q), \\
 i^*_{M}(q) &= \arg\min_{i\in M_{t-1}} d_{\mathrm{eff}}(i,q).
\end{align}
The retention and retrieval gaps are
\begin{align}
 \Delta_{\mathrm{ret}}(q)
 &= d_{\mathrm{eff}}(i^*_{M},q)
    - d_{\mathrm{eff}}(i^*_{\Hset},q), \\
 \Delta_{\mathrm{sel}}(q)
 &= d_{\mathrm{eff}}(R(q;M_{t-1}),q)
    - d_{\mathrm{eff}}(i^*_{M},q).
\end{align}
These quantities separate losing a candidate through eviction from failing to
select a good retained candidate. They use future ground truth and DINO, so
they are offline diagnostic proxies rather than deployable scores or a unique
oracle for useful memory.

\section{Quality-Gated Geometric-Rarity Curation}
\label{sec:policies}

The diagnosis suggests three requirements. First, the bank must remain under a
fixed storage budget. Second, generated observations that disagree with
repeated evidence should not enter the bank. Third, among reliable observations,
the bank must cover camera space without deleting rare scene content. We
therefore propose \method, illustrated in Fig.~\ref{fig:architecture}.

\begin{figure*}[t]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/model_architecture.pdf}
    \caption{\textbf{Proposed model.} New memory items first pass a
    representation-specific reliability adapter. Rare content receives
    protected capacity, while the remaining budget maximizes geometric
    coverage over observed camera demands. The resulting bank is constant in
    size and is consumed by the backbone's existing retriever.}
    \label{fig:architecture}
\end{figure*}

\subsection{Reliability admission without ground truth}

Ground-truth PSNR and SSIM diagnose corruption but are unavailable at inference.
We instead estimate reliability through cross-view consensus. Let
$\mathcal{N}_g(i)$ contain retained observations geometrically co-visible with
candidate $i$, and let $d_{\mathrm{rep}}$ compare their representation after
alignment. For candidates with sufficient support,
\begin{equation}
    \rho_i = \exp\left[-\operatorname{median}_{j\in\mathcal{N}_g(i)}
    d_{\mathrm{rep}}(i,j)\right].
\end{equation}
Supported candidates with $\rho_i<\tau_\rho$ are rejected. Unsupported
candidates are not assumed bad: they remain eligible for rarity protection.
This distinction prevents the quality gate from deleting a genuinely new view
merely because no corroborating observation exists yet.

\subsection{Rare-content protection and geometric coverage}

Let $A_t$ be the reliable union of the previous bank and new candidates. We
cluster representation descriptors and reserve $B_r$ slots for representatives
of the rarest clusters, selecting the most reliable member of each protected
cluster. These slots operationalize RI as a constraint rather than an
arbitrarily weighted term.

The remaining $B-B_r$ slots maximize historical view coverage. Let $Q_t$ be
the camera poses observed up to time $t$, optionally compressed into a bounded
demand reservoir, and let $K_g(q,m)\in[0,1]$ measure geometric coverage. We use
the facility-location objective
\begin{equation}
    F_{\mathrm{geo}}(M)=\sum_{q\in Q_t} w_q\max_{m\in M}K_g(q,m),
    \qquad |M|\leq B,
\end{equation}
initialized with the protected rare representatives and completed by greedy
marginal gain. Only past observations define $Q_t$; no future camera trajectory
is assumed. For heterogeneous memories, $|M|\leq B$ generalizes to
$\sum_{m\in M}c(m)\leq C$, where $c(m)$ is its byte or token cost.

\subsection{Component policies used in ablations}

All component policies are training-free, online, and future-agnostic.
Recently generated anchor frames needed for continuity are pinned; when the
bank exceeds $B$, the selected update rule removes unpinned items.

\paragraph{FIFO.}
FIFO evicts the oldest unpinned frame. It provides a strong recency baseline
but cannot preserve a view that becomes relevant after a long excursion.

\paragraph{Rarity-irreplaceability (RI).}
RI clusters DINO features by thresholding pairwise cosine distance. The
threshold is the median distance to each point's $k$-th nearest neighbor
($k=1$ in the main 180-second runs). For frame $i$ in component $C(i)$ among
$n$ memories, rarity and irreplaceability are
\begin{align}
 r_i &= \log\frac{n+1}{|C(i)|}, \\
 e_i &= \min_{j\neq i} \left\|g_{\mathrm{RGB}}(\hat{x}_i)
                 -g_{\mathrm{RGB}}(\hat{x}_j)\right\|_1, \\
 u_i^{\mathrm{RI}} &= r_i e_i,
\end{align}
where $g_{\mathrm{RGB}}$ is a compact RGB descriptor. RI protects memories
that occupy rare semantic regions and lack a visually similar substitute.

\paragraph{Geometric Coverage.}
This is our geometric-coverage component. A frame covered by many similar
observations is treated as redundant.
For camera-to-world
poses $(R_i,t_i)$ and $(R_j,t_j)$, we compute
\begin{align}
 d_p(i,j) &= \frac{\|t_i-t_j\|_2}{\mathrm{median}_{a\neq b}\|t_a-t_b\|_2}
 + 2\frac{\arccos((\mathrm{tr}(R_i^\top R_j)-1)/2)}{\pi}, \\
 K(i,j) &= 0.65\exp[-d_p(i,j)]
 +0.35\max(\cos(z_i,z_j),0),
\end{align}
where $z_i$ is the normalized DINO feature. Let
$c_i=\sum_{j\neq i}\ind[K(i,j)\geq0.65]$ and
$k_i^{\max}=\max_{j\neq i}K(i,j)$. The retention score is
\begin{equation}
 u_i^{\mathrm{Geo}} =
 1-\min(c_i/3,1)+\frac{0.5}{c_i+1}+0.25(1-k_i^{\max}).
\end{equation}
The policy removes frames already supported by multiple geometrically and
visually similar observations, leaving sparse historical anchors. The full
model adds the reliability gate and protected rarity slots above.

\section{Experiments}

\subsection{Setup}

\paragraph{Generator and data.}
We use the public MemCam implementation built on the Wan2.1 1.3B diffusion
transformer \citep{wan,memcam}. Evaluation uses fifteen held-out trajectories
from the Context-as-Memory dataset \citep{contextasmemory}. Each rollout lasts
180 seconds (5,397 generated frames) and is produced in 76-frame target
sections with 50 denoising steps. Policies share seeds, prompts, trajectories,
and checkpoints.

\paragraph{Policies and budgets.}
The completed diagnostic comparison uses unbounded memory, FIFO, RI, and
Geometric Coverage at $B=32$. The full method combines a reliability admission
gate, protected RI capacity, and Geometric Coverage. We treat FIFO, RI,
Geometric Coverage, K-center, density-balanced coverage, Marginal Coverage
Eviction (MCE), Surprise Forcing, and ungated RI--geometry blends as baselines
or component ablations.
Budgets 16, 32, 64, and 128 test whether conclusions survive changes in bank
capacity. The matched complete $B=32$ grid supports the current 180-second
claim; rows marked \pending{} have not yet been measured under a common
protocol.

\paragraph{Metrics.}
LPIPS compares sampled generated frames with their exact trajectory-indexed
ground truth. FVD uses StyleGAN-V I3D features, 16-frame clips, and the same
sampling configuration for every policy. VBench reports the six dimensions
listed in Table~\ref{tab:vbench}; CUT3R estimates each generated camera path
and reports relative rotation error, scale-aligned translation error, and a
WorldScore-style camera-control score. Efficiency reports archive bytes,
retrieval latency, end-to-end generation latency, and peak GPU memory under
the same device and bank placement. For diagnostic image quality, each
retrieved generated frame is compared with the dataset frame at the same
trajectory index using PSNR and SSIM. Confidence intervals resample the
fifteen trajectories rather than individual retrieval events.

\subsection{What deteriorates as history grows?}

The unbounded bank has zero retention gap by construction, but a mean
selection gap of 0.2267. Across 4,200 audited queries and 1,050 sections,
candidate count correlates positively with selection gap
($\rho=0.203$). Thirteen of fifteen trajectories have a positive within-video
trend (exact sign-test $p=0.0074$). Mean late-minus-early selection gap is
$+0.0723$ with a trajectory-bootstrap 95\% CI of $[0.0396,0.1088]$.

Figure~\ref{fig:pool} resolves a key ambiguity in this growing gap. The
hindsight-best effective mismatch remains approximately flat
($+0.0008$, CI $[-0.0496,0.0546]$), while selected effective mismatch worsens
by $+0.0732$ (CI $[0.0165,0.1296]$). The candidate pool still contains
comparably good historical evidence; the frozen selector fails to surface it.
Candidate count and rollout depth co-vary, so this is a scaling association,
not a causal estimate of pool size alone.

\begin{figure}[t]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/pool_growth.pdf}
    \caption{Unbounded pool-growth diagnostic. Effective mismatch of the
    selected memory increases, while the hindsight-best available candidate
    stays nearly flat. Bins summarize matched 180-second traces.}

The left panel of Fig.~\ref{fig:mechanism} further decomposes this effect.
Selected view mismatch improves by $-0.0335$ (CI

camera-disjoint view. It increasingly selects a plausible view represented by
a damaged generated image.

Geometric Coverage retrieves cleaner memories only because it already
generated a cleaner video.

selected frame indices; the pixels for every index are read from the exact same
unbounded rollout and compared with ground truth.

RI improves PSNR by 1.775 dB (CI $[1.005,2.683]$) and SSIM by 0.0672

\subsection{Does retrieved cleanliness predict the next chunk?}

On each policy's own rollout, selected-memory quality follows the same order:
unbounded obtains 11.703 dB PSNR and 0.3089 SSIM, RI obtains 13.396 and 0.3830,
and Geometric Coverage obtains 16.522 and 0.4759. Relative to unbounded, late
next-chunk SSIM improves by 0.0133 for RI (CI $[0.0028,0.0279]$) and 0.0169
for Geometric Coverage
(CI $[0.0012,0.0392]$). Next-chunk PSNR changes are positive but unresolved:
0.015 (CI $[-0.216,0.271]$) for RI and 0.164
(CI $[-0.111,0.519]$) for Geometric Coverage. The association supports, but
does not prove,
propagation of memory corruption.

A matched one-section context swap provides a preliminary causal check. In one
valid late case, replacing unbounded-selected contexts with Geometric
Coverage-selected
contexts while preserving prior history changes LPIPS by $-0.00177$ and DINO
distance by $-0.01691$. The direction is consistent with the hypothesis but
$n=1$ is intentionally not treated as conclusive evidence.

\section{Discussion}

\paragraph{Curation is quality control.}
The usual systems argument for bounded memory is constant storage and faster
search. Our results establish a model-quality argument: curation alters which
self-generated evidence is repeatedly fed back into the generator. The best
policy is not the one that preserves the greatest number of frames selected by
the unbounded baseline. Indeed, treating unbounded selection as an oracle leads
to the wrong conclusion because that selector is precisely what fails.

\paragraph{Why does Geometric Coverage help?}
Geometric Coverage retains sparse historical anchors and removes memories supported by many
nearby observations. The common-source control shows that this redundancy rule
indirectly favors cleaner indices, although it never explicitly observes PSNR,
SSIM, or ground truth. A plausible explanation is that repeatedly generated
nearby views create dense neighborhoods containing many visually degraded
distractors; pruning redundant neighborhoods reduces their opportunity to win
retrieval. This explanation is consistent with current evidence but still
requires a direct controlled test.

\paragraph{Representation generality.}
The diagnosis applies beyond RGB frames whenever generated memory can become
corrupted: latent tokens can drift, surfels can accumulate appearance or
geometry errors, and point clouds can fuse inconsistent observations. The
specific RI and Geometric Coverage affinities are RGB/pose instantiations, not yet a universal
algorithm. Extending the common-source and cleaning interventions to latent,
surfel, and point-cloud systems is the next test of generality.

\section{Limitations}

Our strongest results use one open generator, one dataset, fifteen trajectories,
and a fixed geometric retriever. FVD is distribution-level and the available
sample does not support per-trajectory significance testing for it. The
hindsight DINO reference is not a true useful-memory oracle and should not be
used as a deployable score. Pool size and elapsed autoregressive time co-vary
in the unbounded rollout. The common-source control establishes cleaner index
selection but does not establish that those pixels cause all downstream quality
gains. A decisive matched cleaning replay must keep selected identities and
history fixed, replacing only generated memory content with trajectory-indexed
ground truth across multiple pre-registered sections. Finally, cross-system
experiments on latent, surfel, and point-cloud memories are required before
claiming representation-independent behavior.

\section{Conclusion}

External memory does not become better merely by becoming larger. In a
long-horizon camera-controlled generator, the complete archive increasingly
exposes a fixed retriever to corrupted self-generated evidence, even while
similarly aligned and cleaner historical candidates remain available. Simple
bounded curation, especially Geometric Coverage, substantially
improves 180-second video quality and consistently selects cleaner indices under
a common-source control. These findings shift memory management from an
implementation detail to part of the generative model's error-control loop.

\section{Policy Probes and Negative Method Results}

We record unsuccessful policy experiments to prevent selection on only positive
results. A density-balanced coverage objective beat Geometric Coverage at 10 seconds but was
worse at 20, 30, and 60 seconds. At 60 seconds, it obtained LPIPS 0.5941 and
FVD 750.3 versus Geometric Coverage's 0.5850 and 690.5. RI with $k=3$ rarity neighborhoods
improved FVD over $k=1$ at 10 seconds (706.7 vs. 713.0) and 20 seconds
(684.6 vs. 721.5), but was worse at 60 seconds (705.9 vs. 660.1). A 50/50
linear blend of normalized RI and Geometric Coverage scores did not beat both constituents.
These failures argue against an uncalibrated weighted sum as a final method.

\section{Ground-Truth Cleaning Replay Protocol}

The planned causal intervention is deliberately local. For a pre-registered
late section, replay the exact unbounded history and selected frame identities
in two branches. The control branch uses the generated selected images. The
clean branch replaces only those images with dataset ground truth at the same
indices. Both branches then generate one section from identical latent noise.
The paired difference in target-indexed LPIPS, SSIM, and DINO distance estimates
the immediate effect of corrupted memory content while holding view identity,
history, model, and randomness fixed. Four high-corruption cases were selected
with mean corruption 0.9839, 0.8981, 0.8609, and 0.8249; complete paired results
were not available at manuscript time.

\section{Scaling of the Current Implementation}

With $s$ generated frames added per chunk, an unbounded RGB archive uses
$O(Ts)$ storage after $T$ chunks and exhaustive overlap retrieval incurs
$O(T^2s)$ cumulative candidate comparisons. A fixed bank uses $O(B)$ storage
and $O(TB)$ cumulative retrieval. Implementation-derived estimates, rather
than direct long-duration measurements, project 2.30 GB of bank storage and
12 minutes of retrieval at 60 seconds, 22.7 GB and 20.9 hours at 10 minutes,
and 136 GB and 31.5 days at 60 minutes. These projections are hardware- and
implementation-dependent and are not used to support the quality claims.
