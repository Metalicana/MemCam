\title{\textbf{The Archive Is Not the Context:}\\
Diagnosing and Curating Memory for Long-Horizon Video Generation}
\author{Anonymous authors}
\date{}

\begin{document}

\begin{abstract}
Long-horizon video generators increasingly store generated frames or latent
states in an external archive and retrieve a small context for each new video
chunk. Existing work has largely optimized the read operation while allowing
the candidate archive to grow with duration. We show that this distinction
matters: the generator consumes the retrieved context, not the archive itself.
An expanding archive incurs linear storage and quadratic cumulative exhaustive
search, and can make an imperfect retriever select worse conditioning evidence.
On fifteen matched 180-second MemCam rollouts, a 32-frame geometric-coverage
bank reduces FVD from 734.2 to 476.6 and LPIPS from 0.5980 to 0.5876 relative
to retaining all 5,397 frames. FIFO-32 worsens LPIPS, showing that capacity
reduction alone is insufficient. We separate memory failure into retention and
selection gaps, and further decompose selected evidence into target-view
mismatch and stored-image corruption. As history grows, selected views become
slightly better aligned, but their generated content becomes substantially
more corrupted. A common-source control holds all historical pixels fixed and
changes only selected indices; geometric coverage chooses memories that are
4.63 dB cleaner in PSNR and 0.151 higher in SSIM than unbounded selection,
winning on all fifteen trajectories. A matched WorldMem study shows the same
high-level phenomenon for latent memory. Controlled comparisons of coverage,
rarity, recency, generic coresets, and reliability signals show that geometric
coverage is the strongest observable retention principle in the studied
systems, while generated-image reliability is important in hindsight but
poorly predictable at write time. The resulting paper is a diagnosis of
candidate competition and an empirical study of what a fixed-budget generative
memory should preserve.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Long-horizon video generation turns a finite-context model into a recurrent
system. The generator produces a chunk, stores information from that chunk,
and retrieves historical information to condition the next one. This pattern
appears in RGB-frame memory \citep{contextasmemory,memcam}, learned latent
memory \citep{worldmem,mirage}, surfel-indexed view memory \citep{vmem}, and
point-cloud memory \citep{spatia}. External memory helps recover objects and
scene regions after they leave the field of view, but it introduces a second
problem: what should remain available when accumulated history exceeds the
useful memory budget?

The common default is complete retention. Storage and candidate-search cost
then grow with duration, although the generator still receives only a small
retrieved context. More history therefore does not provide more direct
conditioning capacity. It provides more candidates competing for the same
read slots. When retrieval score is imperfectly aligned with downstream
conditioning quality, additional candidates can increase the chance that an
apparently relevant but degraded memory wins.

We study this problem in MemCam \citep{memcam}, an open camera-controlled
generator that stores decoded RGB frames and retrieves historical frames by
camera field-of-view overlap. We leave the generator and retriever unchanged
and intervene only on the candidate archive. Fixed-budget curation
substantially outperforms complete retention at long horizons. At 180 seconds,
rarity--irreplaceability (\RI) improves FVD from 734.2 to 550.4, while a
SLAM-inspired Geometric Coverage intervention (\Geo) reaches 476.6 with only
32 stored frames. FIFO at the same capacity performs poorly, so ``less
memory'' is not the explanation; the retained structure matters.

Our central goal is to explain this counter-intuitive result. We distinguish
two errors: \emph{retention loss}, when budgeting removes the best available
historical evidence, and \emph{selection loss}, when the frozen retriever fails
among candidates that remain. We then separate whether a selected index
depicts the requested view from whether the generated image stored at that
index is faithful. The selected view becomes slightly better aligned as the
archive grows, while selected generated content becomes worse. Thus the
failure is not direct attention dilution over archive tokens, nor primarily a
camera-disjoint retrieval. It is increasing exposure to corrupted historical
evidence through candidate competition.

We evaluate three retention principles rather than manufacturing a composite
``full model'': geometric coverage, rare-mode preservation, and observable
reliability. Geometric coverage is the strongest online signal in MemCam and
WorldMem. Rarity is useful but less aligned with the downstream retriever.
Reliability is strongly associated with quality in hindsight, but generic
no-reference IQA, pose-conditioned consistency, and hard same-view hysteresis
all fail as deployable interventions. These negative results are part of the
scientific conclusion.

Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item We formalize candidate competition under a finite read budget and
    show how adding distractors monotonically lowers the probability that a
    useful candidate wins under an imperfect retrieval score.
    \item We introduce a diagnostic decomposition of retention, selection,
    target-view mismatch, and stored-image corruption for generative memory.
    \item Through matched 180-second rollouts and a common-source control, we
    show that bounded curation can select cleaner evidence than complete
    retention while using below one percent of the archive.
    \item We compare controlled retention principles and report failed
    reliability interventions, then replicate the principal bounded-memory
    advantage in WorldMem's latent-memory interface.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{Long-horizon generation with memory.}
Context-as-Memory stores historical RGB frames and retrieves them using camera
field-of-view overlap \citep{contextasmemory}. MemCam adds context compression
and provides an open implementation of RGB-frame retrieval \citep{memcam}.
WorldMem stores visual memories with pose and timestamp states and reads them
through learned memory attention \citep{worldmem}. VMem indexes past views
using surfels \citep{vmem}; Spatia maintains an updatable point cloud
\citep{spatia}; Mirage stores spatial evidence in diffusion latent space
\citep{mirage}; and AnchorWeave retrieves multiple local geometric memories
\citep{anchorweave}. These methods primarily study memory representation and
reading. We study candidate retention under a fixed budget.

\paragraph{Keyframes and bounded memory.}
SLAM systems use co-visibility graphs and keyframe culling to remove redundant
observations while maintaining a map for localization and reconstruction
\citep{orbslam,orbslam2}. Our Geometric Coverage intervention explicitly
adapts this principle; we do not claim to invent co-visibility. The setting and
objective differ: stored images are generated and potentially corrupted, the
memory conditions a diffusion model rather than a geometric optimizer, and
quality is measured on the resulting video. Surprise Forcing instead treats
memory admission as feedback-controlled online allocation
\citep{surpriseforcing}. Generic subset-selection methods offer diversity and
coverage objectives \citep{nemhauser1978,badanidiyuru2014}; we include
K-center and a marginal-coverage policy as controlled baselines.

\paragraph{Video-generation evaluation.}
We report LPIPS \citep{lpips} and FVD \citep{fvd}. VBench \citep{vbench}
measures subject and background consistency, motion, dynamics, aesthetics, and
imaging quality. PSNR and SSIM \citep{ssim} are used only when exact
trajectory-indexed ground truth makes pixel comparison meaningful. DINOv2
features \citep{dinov2} support appearance diagnostics, but DINO distance is
not treated as ground-truth memory utility. We do not report CUT3R
\citep{cut3r} results because the current evaluator failed its ground-truth
camera sanity check.

\section{Archive Growth Under a Finite Read Budget}
\label{sec:problem}

\subsection{Streaming formulation}

At chunk $t$, let $Y_t$ denote the frames to generate, $P_t$ the camera poses
known for that chunk, and $M_{t-1}$ the persistent memory. A retriever $R$
maps each target to a small context:
\begin{align}
    C_t &= R(P_t;M_{t-1}), \\
    \hat{Y}_t &= G(\hat{Y}_{t-1},P_t,C_t), \\
    M_t &= U(M_{t-1},N_t;B),
\end{align}
where $N_t$ contains newly generated candidates and $B$ is the archive
budget. Unbounded memory appends every candidate. The future trajectory beyond
the current chunk is unavailable to $U$.

If every chunk adds $s$ candidates, complete retention uses $O(Ts)$ storage
after $T$ chunks. Exhaustive retrieval against the growing history costs
$O(T^2s)$ cumulative candidate comparisons. A fixed bank uses $O(B)$ storage
and $O(TB)$ cumulative retrieval. These are horizon-scaling results; the cost
of updating a particular fixed-budget policy is separate.
Figure~\ref{fig:problem} places these scaling laws beside the measured
duration-dependent quality trend.

    decoded-bank storage is linear and cumulative exhaustive retrieval is

    extrapolations. Right: measured unbounded LPIPS worsens with duration,
    while FVD remains high and non-monotonic.}

\end{figure*}

\subsection{Candidate competition}

Archive growth also changes the statistical decision faced by the retriever.
Consider one useful historical candidate with retrieval score $S_c$ and $n$
distractors with scores $S_1,\ldots,S_n$. The score need only be imperfectly
aligned with downstream conditioning quality; a distractor may score highly
because it matches camera geometry while containing corrupted generated
content.

\begin{proposition}[Candidate competition]
Assume distractor scores are conditionally independent with CDF $F_d$. The
probability that the useful candidate wins top-1 retrieval is
\begin{equation}
 P_{\mathrm{win}}(n)=\mathbb{E}_{S_c}\left[F_d(S_c)^n\right],
 \label{eq:competition}
\end{equation}
and is non-increasing in $n$.
\end{proposition}

\noindent
For every $s$, $0\leq F_d(s)\leq1$, hence
$F_d(s)^{n+1}\leq F_d(s)^n$; expectation preserves the inequality. The result
does not say that every added memory is harmful. It says that adding
competing draws creates more opportunities for a high-scoring distractor to
win when retrieval score and conditioning quality disagree. It also predicts
that geometric match can improve while selected-content fidelity worsens,
which is the pattern observed in Sec.~\ref{sec:mechanism}.
Equation~\ref{eq:competition} is a minimal model rather than a fitted law for
MemCam; the experiments below test its qualitative prediction.

\section{What Should a Fixed-Budget Memory Preserve?}
\label{sec:principles}

We keep the generator, retriever, prompts, seeds, and camera trajectories
fixed, and vary only $U$. This turns memory policies into controlled
interventions rather than a new generation backbone.

\subsection{Recency}

FIFO removes the oldest unpinned memory. It controls for capacity and favors
the immediately preceding trajectory segment, but cannot retain a view that
becomes relevant after a long excursion.

\subsection{Rare-mode preservation}

Rarity--irreplaceability clusters DINO features by thresholding pairwise
cosine distance. The threshold is the median distance to each point's
$k$-th-nearest neighbor. For frame $i$ in connected component $C(i)$ among
$n$ memories,
\begin{align}
 r_i &= \log\frac{n+1}{|C(i)|}, \\
 e_i &= \min_{j\neq i}\left\|g_{\mathrm{RGB}}(\hat{x}_i)
                    -g_{\mathrm{RGB}}(\hat{x}_j)\right\|_1, \\
 u_i^{\mathrm{RI}} &= r_i e_i .
\end{align}
Here $g_{\mathrm{RGB}}$ is a compact RGB descriptor. A corrected $k=3$
calibration is used in the final 60-second ablation; the canonical 180-second
run predates that correction and is labeled accordingly. Rarity-only removes
$e_i$ and evicts items from large DINO components first.

\subsection{Geometric coverage}

Our strongest intervention adapts SLAM-style co-visibility culling. For
camera-to-world poses $(R_i,t_i)$ and $(R_j,t_j)$, define
\begin{align}
 d_p(i,j) &=
 \frac{\|t_i-t_j\|_2}
 {\mathrm{median}_{a\neq b}\|t_a-t_b\|_2}
 +2\frac{\arccos((\mathrm{tr}(R_i^\top R_j)-1)/2)}{\pi}, \\
 K(i,j) &= 0.65\exp[-d_p(i,j)]
 +0.35\max(\cos(z_i,z_j),0),
 \label{eq:affinity}
\end{align}
where $z_i$ is a normalized DINO feature. Let
$c_i=\sum_{j\neq i}\ind[K(i,j)\geq0.65]$ and
$k_i^{\max}=\max_{j\neq i}K(i,j)$. The retention utility is
\begin{equation}
 u_i^{\mathrm{Geo}}=
 1-\min(c_i/3,1)+\frac{0.5}{c_i+1}+0.25(1-k_i^{\max}).
 \label{eq:geocov}
\end{equation}
Frames with at least three co-visible observers and a close substitute receive
low utility. The buffer evicts the lowest utilities until $B$ items remain.
This is a local redundancy criterion, not an optimal set-coverage theorem.

\subsection{Generic set coverage and reliability}

K-center retains descriptor-space centers. Marginal Coverage Eviction uses a
noisy-OR facility objective, while density-balanced coverage weights candidate
regions before computing marginal loss. These policies test whether generic
set coverage is sufficient.

Reliability asks a different question: can a corrupted generated observation
be recognized before it becomes persistent evidence? We test generic
no-reference IQA, pose-calibrated agreement with the actual conditioning
context, and same-view hysteresis. Ground-truth fidelity is used only as a
held-out label, never as a policy input. None becomes part of the final policy
because none passes both held-out discrimination and rollout quality.

\section{Experimental Setup}

\paragraph{MemCam.}
We use the public MemCam implementation built on the Wan2.1 1.3B diffusion
transformer \citep{wan,memcam}. The primary study uses fifteen matched
180-second trajectories from the Context-as-Memory dataset
\citep{contextasmemory}. Each rollout contains 5,397 frames generated in
76-frame target sections with 50 denoising steps. Policies share checkpoints,
prompts, trajectories, and random seeds. The primary budget is $B=32$.

\paragraph{WorldMem.}
We separately evaluate the first fifteen matched 60-second WorldMem rollouts.
WorldMem uses a latent-memory interface and a different generator-facing read
mechanism \citep{worldmem}. The purpose is to test whether complete retention
is also dominated outside MemCam, not whether one RGB kernel transfers
unchanged. We report within-system comparisons only.

\paragraph{Metrics.}
LPIPS compares sampled generated frames with exact trajectory-indexed ground
truth. FVD uses the same StyleGAN-V I3D detector, 16-frame clips, four clips
per video, stride four, and 224-pixel inputs within each comparison. Standard
VBench uses identical fifteen-video subsets. PSNR and SSIM compare a selected
generated frame with dataset ground truth at the same trajectory index.
Confidence intervals resample trajectories. CUT3R and VBench-Long are excluded:
the former failed a ground-truth camera sanity check and the latter did not run
in the available environment. We prefer an explicit missing measurement to an
invalid number.

\subsection{Retention and selection are different objectives}
\label{sec:decomposition}

The dataset provides ground-truth image $x_i^{\GT}$ at trajectory index $i$,
while memory stores generated image $\hat{x}_i$. For target $q$ and memory
index $i$, define
\begin{align}
 d_{\mathrm{view}}(i,q) &= d_{\DINO}(x_i^{\GT},x_q^{\GT}), \\
 d_{\mathrm{corr}}(i) &= d_{\DINO}(\hat{x}_i,x_i^{\GT}), \\
 d_{\mathrm{eff}}(i,q) &= d_{\DINO}(\hat{x}_i,x_q^{\GT}).
\end{align}
For diagnosis only, let $i^*_{\Hset}$ be the minimum-effective-mismatch item
in complete history and $i^*_M$ the minimum in the current bank. If $i_R$ is
the item chosen by the real retriever,
\begin{align}
 \Delta_{\mathrm{ret}} &= d_{\mathrm{eff}}(i^*_M,q)
                         -d_{\mathrm{eff}}(i^*_{\Hset},q), \\
 \Delta_{\mathrm{sel}} &= d_{\mathrm{eff}}(i_R,q)
                         -d_{\mathrm{eff}}(i^*_M,q).
\end{align}
These are hindsight DINO diagnostics, not true utility labels.

Unbounded preserves every historical possibility and therefore has zero

beats unbounded on 13/15 ($p=0.0074$). Retaining potentially useful evidence
and making retained evidence retrievable are different objectives.

\subsection{What deteriorates as the archive grows?}
\label{sec:mechanism}

Across 4,200 audited unbounded queries and 1,050 sections, candidate count
correlates with selection gap ($\rho=0.203$). Thirteen of fifteen trajectories
have a positive trend (sign-test $p=0.0074$). Mean late-minus-early selection
gap is $+0.0723$ with trajectory-bootstrap 95\% CI
$[0.0396,0.1088]$. The hindsight-best effective mismatch remains flat
($+0.0008$, CI $[-0.0496,0.0546]$), while selected mismatch worsens by
$+0.0732$ (CI $[0.0165,0.1296]$). Candidate count and elapsed generation time
co-vary, so this is a scaling association rather than a causal estimate of
pool size alone.
Figure~\ref{fig:pool} shows that the selected candidate worsens while the best
available diagnostic candidate remains approximately flat.

Selected view mismatch improves by $-0.0335$ (CI

are imperfectly aligned.

the best signal would reject roughly 37 bad and 100 clean frames per 1,000
candidates. Pose-conditioned causal consistency was near chance and worse than
raw similarity. Both predeclared decisions were \textsc{do not inject}.

An offline same-view test did reveal a population tendency: at camera-view
threshold 0.90, an older representative was cleaner than a later rewrite by
0.860 dB PSNR and 0.0397 SSIM across 823 pairs. The effect remained positive
from thresholds 0.80 to 0.95. However, converting it into hard hysteresis
worsened 60-second FVD from 690.5 to 768.7 and lost four meaningful VBench
dimensions. A true retrospective property need not be a useful online control
signal. The hard rule over-preserved incumbents and blocked useful refresh.

\section{Discussion}

\paragraph{The contribution is not a new SLAM rule.}
Equations~\ref{eq:affinity}--\ref{eq:geocov} deliberately adapt keyframe
redundancy ideas. Their
value here is as a controlled intervention into generative memory. The paper's
scientific contribution is the candidate-competition formulation, the
retention/selection decomposition, and evidence that complete retention can
degrade a fixed read interface. Renaming a SLAM-inspired score would not make
its ingredients novel, so we state the lineage explicitly.

\paragraph{Why does a geometric policy address a corruption diagnosis?}
\Geo does not estimate image quality. It intervenes earlier in the chain:
archive growth creates dense populations of near-equivalent candidates;
candidate competition lets a geometrically convincing but corrupted item win;
redundancy culling reduces that population. The common-source control is the
empirical bridge: with identical historical pixels, \Geo changes only the
candidate indices and selects substantially cleaner evidence. We therefore
describe corrupted selected content as the consequence and geometric curation
as a candidate-set intervention, not as a quality detector.

\paragraph{Retention, retrievability, and reliability.}
The experiments expose three distinct objectives. \RI best preserves rare
historical modes under the hindsight diagnostic. \Geo best supports the frozen
retriever. Reliability is associated with downstream quality but cannot be
predicted accurately enough from tested causal observables. A useful memory
system may eventually optimize all three, but our evidence does not justify a
weighted sum or quality gate as the final method.

\paragraph{Representation dependence.}
MemCam and WorldMem both benefit from structured bounded memory, but their
interfaces differ. RGB, latent, surfel, and point-cloud systems need different
affinities and item costs. The transferable claim is the need to separate the
archive from the retrieved context and evaluate curation against the actual
read interface. The concrete kernel is system-specific.

\section{Limitations}

The primary mechanism study uses one open generator, one dataset, fifteen
trajectories, and MemCam's geometric retriever. The WorldMem result supports a
second interface but does not provide the complete VBench and camera metric
matrix. FVD is distribution-level and the current sample does not permit
per-trajectory significance testing. Pool size and autoregressive age co-vary,
so the pool-growth association is not a controlled causal effect of candidate
count. The hindsight DINO reference is not a true useful-memory oracle.

The common-source experiment proves that curation changes index quality under
fixed pixels, but the multi-case ground-truth memory-cleaning replay did not
complete; one valid context-swap case improved LPIPS by only 0.00177 and is
underpowered. We therefore do not claim that the measured corruption fully
causes downstream FVD. We also do not know the privileged $B=32$ oracle
headroom: a future clean/future-view oracle could reveal whether \Geo is near
the capacity ceiling or far from it. Finally, our complexity argument concerns
archive storage and retrieval; fixed-budget update cost depends on policy
implementation.

\section{Conclusion}

More external memory is not automatically better. A long-horizon generator
reads a small retrieved context, and an expanding archive can make that read
problem harder while increasing storage and search cost. In MemCam, complete
retention preserves every historical option but produces the largest measured
selection gap. Structured 32-frame banks, especially geometric coverage,
generate better 180-second video and select cleaner evidence even under a
common-source control. WorldMem shows the same broad advantage for latent
memory. Coverage, rarity, and reliability emerge as separate retention
principles: geometry is the strongest tested online signal, rarity preserves
different modes, and reliability remains difficult to infer causally. These
findings place memory curation inside the generative error-control problem
rather than treating it as an implementation detail.

\section{Negative Policy Results}

Density-balanced coverage obtains LPIPS/FVD 0.5941/750.3 at 60 seconds versus
0.5850/690.5 for \Geo. A 50/50 \Geo--\RI blend does not beat both
constituents. Rarity-only and the two 75/25 blends are reported in
Table~\ref{tab:rarity}. Coverage hysteresis improves several early-prefix
cells but fails badly at 60-second FVD. MCE, trajectory coverage, and direct
facility-style objectives likewise fail to improve the complete metric suite.
We report these outcomes to avoid selecting only favorable policies after the
fact.

\section{Preliminary Causal Replay}

One valid context-identity replay holds model, prompt, trajectory, seed,
unbounded bank, and preceding selected contexts fixed, then substitutes
Geometric Coverage-selected contexts at one late section. The substitution
changes LPIPS by $-0.00177$ and DINO distance by $-0.01691$. The direction is
consistent with the proposed mechanism, but $n=1$ cannot support a causal
claim. A stronger planned intervention replaces selected generated content
with exact-index ground truth while preserving identities; incomplete cluster
runs prevent us from reporting it.

\section{Implementation Scaling}

The current decoded RGB archive occupies approximately 0.38 GB at 10 seconds
and 2.30 GB at 60 seconds. Implementation-derived extrapolations reach 22.7 GB
at 10 minutes and 136 GB at 60 minutes. Corresponding exhaustive retrieval
estimates are 12 minutes, 20.9 hours, and 31.5 days. These long-duration values
are extrapolations tied to the current implementation and hardware; they are
used to illustrate scaling, not as direct runtime measurements.
